# Ledger: explicit decoder versions

Part of the [compatibility matrix](../compatibility.md); the register entry is `ledger-decoder-version` in [compatibility.json](../data/compatibility.json). The sections below record the implementation, evidence and remaining limits.

## Status as recorded

implemented: the reference decodes at an explicit `Version` with no check against the type it reads (`decodeFull'`, `libs/cardano-ledger-binary/src/Cardano/Ledger/Binary/Decoding.hs:93-100`, cardano-ledger `f649f975`); consensus reads a block of an era at its `eraProtVerLow`. `TxBody.decode`, `Witnesses.decode`, `Tx.fromEnvelope`, `Tx.fromBlock`, `Certificate.decode` and `Header.fromRaw` take a version as an optional last argument, and `Block.decode` and `Block.decodeShelley` one per era, and read every version-dependent part at it, as given. Without one they read at `eraProtVerLow`, as before. `DecoderVersion.at(era, major)` builds a version of an era's range, `ProtVerLow` to `ProtVerHigh` (`libs/cardano-ledger-core/internal/Cardano/Ledger/Internal/Definition/Era.hs:87-141`: Byron 0 to 1, Alonzo 5 to 6, Babbage 7 to 8, Conway 9 to 11, one version for each other era), and refuses any other with the typed `OutOfRange`. At `f649f975` no decoder branches at a version above an era's lowest and within its range (6, 8, 10 or 11; the branches are at 2, 3, 5, 7, 9 and 12), so every version of an era gives the verdict and the value its lowest gives; a refusal of an auxiliary Plutus script before its language's version names the version it was read at.

## Evidence

`DecoderVersionSuite`: the range of every era against the reference's table; every version of each Shelley-based era's range reads the era-decoders, recd-detached, recd-records, tx-envelope and header-decoding corpora (599 lines) with the same result as the era's own version; and each place a version reaches, read across the branch it meets through its entry point: at 9 a body's inputs, withdrawals and certificates, a certificate's pool owners and anchor url, a witness set's vkey list, a transaction's body and metadata labels (enveloped and from a block), and a block body's auxiliary data; at 12 a witness set's chain code, a header's chunked byte string, and the detached records of a block, a body, a witness set and a transaction. `BootstrapDuplicatesReferenceSuite` and `BootstrapChainCodesReferenceSuite` hold the reference's verdict at every decoder version from 2 to 12 and now read their lines at Alonzo 6, Babbage 8 and Conway 10 and 11 at that version. The per-era fuzz targets of bodies, witness sets, transactions and headers compare every version of the era's range.

## Explicitly unchecked or unconfirmed

Byron's range is the reference's 0 to 1, while the decoders read a Byron era's Shelley-shaped parts at Shelley's 2 by default (`DecoderVersion.of`), as before. A part of an era read at a version outside its range is read by that era's decoders with the branches they implement; the branches at 12 that no era of theirs reads at (for example `decodeAnnSet`'s refusal of a repeated element) are not implemented, and the bootstrap corpora's lines at such versions are not compared. The era-derived choices (address strictness, the newest script language, the output forms) stay with the era, where some of the reference's are made by version. Apart from the two bootstrap corpora, the versions above an era's lowest are compared with Scarab's own reading at the lowest, not with the reference run at them. `Block.decode` and `decodeShelley` with a version have no fuzz target of their own. Dijkstra's range is one version, and its transactions are read by the readers of `dijkstra.V12`, which take no version. Node-to-client query arguments (`NtcQuery`), the bootstrap's reading of block bodies (`BootstrapLedger`), the rules' second reading of auxiliary scripts (`TxChecks`) and the empty redeemers of the integrity hash (`ScriptIntegrity`) stay at `eraProtVerLow`.
