# Ledger: explicit decoder versions within an era's range

Part of the [compatibility matrix](../compatibility.md); the register entry is `ledger-decoder-version` in [compatibility.json](../data/compatibility.json). The sections below record the implementation, evidence and remaining limits.

## Status as recorded

implemented: the reference decodes at an explicit `Version` (`decodeFull'`, cardano-ledger-binary); consensus reads a block of an era at its `eraProtVerLow`, and a reader may name any version. `DecoderVersion.at(era, major)` builds a version of the era's range, `ProtVerLow` to `ProtVerHigh` (`libs/cardano-ledger-core/internal/Cardano/Ledger/Internal/Definition/Era.hs:87-141`, cardano-ledger `f649f975`: Alonzo 5 to 6, Babbage 7 to 8, Conway 9 to 11, one version for each other era), and refuses any other with the typed `OutOfRange`. `TxBody.decode`, `Witnesses.decode`, `Tx.fromEnvelope`, `Tx.fromBlock`, `Certificate.decode` and `Header.fromRaw` take one as an optional last argument, and `Block.decode` and `Block.decodeShelley` one per era; each refuses a version outside its era (`LedgerDecodeError.VersionOutsideEra`) and reads every version-dependent part at it. Without one they read at `eraProtVerLow`, as before. At `f649f975` no decoder branches at a version above an era's lowest and within its range (6, 8, 10 or 11; the branches are at 2, 3, 5, 7, 9 and 12), so every version of an era gives the verdict and the value its lowest gives; a refusal of an auxiliary Plutus script before its language's version names the version it was read at.

## Evidence

`DecoderVersionSuite`: the range of every era against the reference's table; every version of each Shelley-based era's range reads the era-decoders, recd-detached, recd-records, tx-envelope and header-decoding corpora (599 lines) with the same result as the era's own version; refusals of versions outside an era at every entry point; and, read at a neighbouring era's version through the readers the entry points share, the branch at 9 (duplicate inputs through a body and a transaction, duplicate pool owners through a certificate, a repeated auxiliary-data index through a block body) and the branch at 12 (a 31-byte chain code through a witness set). `BootstrapDuplicatesReferenceSuite` and `BootstrapChainCodesReferenceSuite` hold the reference's verdict at every decoder version from 2 to 12 and now read their lines at Alonzo 6, Babbage 8 and Conway 10 and 11 at that version. The per-era fuzz targets of bodies, witness sets, transactions and headers compare every version of the era's range.

## Explicitly unchecked or unconfirmed

Byron's range is Shelley's, 2 to 2, because Scarab reads Byron's parts of these shapes as Shelley's (`DecoderVersion.of`); the reference's Byron range is 0 to 1. An entry point refuses a version of another era, where `decodeFull'` would read an era's type at any version. Apart from the two bootstrap corpora, the versions above an era's lowest are compared with Scarab's own reading at the lowest, not with the reference run at them. `Block.decode` and `decodeShelley` with a version have no fuzz target of their own. Dijkstra's range is one version, and its transactions are read by the readers of `dijkstra.V12`, which take no version. Node-to-client query arguments (`NtcQuery`), the bootstrap's reading of block bodies (`BootstrapLedger`), the rules' second reading of auxiliary scripts (`TxChecks`) and the empty redeemers of the integrity hash (`ScriptIntegrity`) stay at `eraProtVerLow`.
