# Roadmap

What remains after the v0.1.0 research preview. Item IDs (A1, B3, ...) are stable: issues and pull
requests reference them, and they are not renumbered when items are done (done items are marked, not
deleted). Priorities:

- **P0** is needed for the research preview or blocks collaborators.
- **P1** is needed for a full reference-compatible node.
- **P2** is hardening or nice-to-have.

Each open item links to its GitHub issue; the pinned [tracking issue #64](https://github.com/KD5UJC-XYZ/scarab/issues/64) groups them. How each area stands today is in [compatibility.md](compatibility.md).

## D. Scale (P1)

- **D1** [#20](https://github.com/KD5UJC-XYZ/scarab/issues/20) More state on disk: accounts, pools, DReps and stake snapshots behind stores (only the UTxO is on disk today).
- **D2** [#21](https://github.com/KD5UJC-XYZ/scarab/issues/21) Parallel or optimised apply. The apply thread is sequential (rules, Plutus, leader threshold).
- **D3** [#22](https://github.com/KD5UJC-XYZ/scarab/issues/22) (P1, done) ChainSync pipelining depth. The 256 timeout was not reproduced: 16 runs of 12 to 15 minutes against the public Preview relay at depths 64, 128, 200 and 256 (base build, this build, header-first on and off, and the pre-D8 scheduling on 2 CPUs) had no timeout; the upstream limits do not forbid 256 (reference client high mark 300, ChainSync ingress queue 462,000 bytes against 2 bytes per request, `shortWait` 10 s, the same as ours) and the relay answers a 256-header round in 0.26 s on average, 0.8 s at most. What was found and fixed: the receive path was quadratic (every byte read through a `Vector` of the whole buffer), 0.7 MB/s on 100 blocks of 90 KB, which on the connection shared with BlockFetch can hold ChainSync replies past the 10 s wait; 48 MB/s now. The default `--read-ahead` is **128** (64 is 6 to 10% slower, 128 to 256 are equal); the node logs the round times (`chainSyncRounds`) and slow single-block fetches (`slowFetch`). Details and numbers: [performance.md](performance.md) section 7. Remaining: the chain regions with large blocks (mainnet) were not reached; the reference client keeps up to 300 requests outstanding and collects down to 200, ours sends a round and collects all of it.
- **D4** [#23](https://github.com/KD5UJC-XYZ/scarab/issues/23) (done) Volatile blocks. The last k blocks are logged (`VolatileStore`) and applied again on start, and a graceful stop snapshots the immutable tip. Tested: restart equivalence with and without the log, a stop followed by a start that replays 0 blocks, copies of a running database at 12 points, the log torn at every record boundary; Preview restarts in [performance.md](performance.md). Remaining: no fault injection at the filesystem level (only truncated and flipped bytes), and a SIGKILL during the shutdown snapshot is the ordinary `kill -9` case.
- **D5** [#24](https://github.com/KD5UJC-XYZ/scarab/issues/24) (done) DB network identity: `<db>/network-identity` (magic, Shelley and Byron genesis hashes) is written at creation and checked on open (`StorageError.NetworkMismatch`); a database without it is adopted when its first block names the configured genesis. Remaining: the Alonzo and Conway genesis hashes are verified against the configuration (J1) but not part of the identity, and on a Shelley-start network the adoption check cannot tell two networks apart.
- **D6** [#25](https://github.com/KD5UJC-XYZ/scarab/issues/25) Mainnet sync. About 12M blocks: measure throughput and memory, then optimise.
- **D7** [#26](https://github.com/KD5UJC-XYZ/scarab/issues/26) Ed25519 throughput. Only parallelism is used. A faster path that can only accept was correctly rejected as a security weakening, so any speedup must keep strict semantics.
- **D8** [#65](https://github.com/KD5UJC-XYZ/scarab/issues/65) (P1, done, with the caveats below; numbers in [performance.md](performance.md) section 6) Scheduling fairness: the peer session runs on its own work-stealing pool (`Compute[F]`, `--cpu-threads`) instead of the cats-effect compute pool; timer lateness and the longest CPU-bound step per epoch are measured and logged (`scheduling` on the epoch lines, `GET /scheduling`). Evidence: Preview from genesis for 31 min, started together with the base build, -3.2% blocks at 6 CPUs and +0.2% at 1 CPU, 0 starvation warnings in both; devnet `pool --scala-cpus 1` 0 missed leader slots in 11 of 14 runs (the base build: 5 of 6); the others missed 16 to 19 of 69 to 88 scheduled slots because the node's slot clock was 1.0 s behind the network's, cause not found, also seen on the base build (the pool summary now reports `slotClock`). Remaining: the 1-CPU starvation warnings were the serial collector's pauses and need `-XX:+UseG1GC -XX:MaxGCPauseMillis=20` (the base build has none either with those flags, so the pool is not what removed them); a step is one uninterrupted pure call (the longest is reported, not bounded: 167 ms at 6 CPUs, 512 ms at 1 CPU); the mempool revalidation and the forger still run on the compute pool; no Prometheus metric (J3).
- **D9** [#69](https://github.com/KD5UJC-XYZ/scarab/issues/69) (P1) Mainnet: start from a Mithril snapshot (E1), follow to the tip and stay there for a week, comparing the ledger at every epoch boundary (K7).

## G. Networking (P1/P2)

- **G1** [#31](https://github.com/KD5UJC-XYZ/scarab/issues/31) P2P governor: root, ledger, big-ledger and bootstrap peers; churn; PeerSharing.
- **G2** [#32](https://github.com/KD5UJC-XYZ/scarab/issues/32) (P1, done) Connection management: `ConnectionState` (pure life cycle), `ConnectionManager` (one connection per peer address, inbound and outbound limits, typed refusals, release on failure and cancellation) and `InboundGovernor`, which runs our ChainSync, BlockFetch and KeepAlive clients (the pipelined, header-validating peer, with the follower session) on every inbound duplex connection, so a peer that connected to us is a block source. Outbound connections go through the manager and wait for a peer's inbound duplex connection instead of dialling a second one. Evidence: `ConnectionManagerSuite`, `ConnectionStateSuite`, `InboundGovernorSuite` (in-memory), and the devnet `relay` scenario (the Haskell relay connects to the Scala node and the Scala node follows the relay over that connection; `GET /connections`). Remaining: connections we open offer initiator-only (a peer we dial is not served over that connection, so its own inbound connection is never refused because ours is up and the peer is then followed over both until one ends); no connection pruning or churn (G1); a peer that dials from an ephemeral port is not recognised as the peer we also dial; no per-peer resource limits (G5).
- **G3** [#33](https://github.com/KD5UJC-XYZ/scarab/issues/33) Ouroboros Genesis (GDD, LoE/LoP).
- **G4** [#34](https://github.com/KD5UJC-XYZ/scarab/issues/34) Protocol versions: handshake v15/v16 features; NtC versions.
- **G5** [#35](https://github.com/KD5UJC-XYZ/scarab/issues/35) Adversarial robustness: resource limits per peer, slow-loris and oversized-message handling, fuzzing the mini-protocol state machines.
- **G6** [#36](https://github.com/KD5UJC-XYZ/scarab/issues/36) (P1, done) Header-first validation: each ChainSync batch is validated before any of its blocks is fetched (`HeaderGate`, `NodeHeaderRules`): envelope, VRF, leader threshold, KES, opcert and counter, nonces along the candidate header chain, against a ledger view forecast from the newest ledger state of ours on the peer's chain within the stability window; an invalid header ends the session with `PeerError.InvalidHeader` and no block of the batch is fetched. Signature verdicts are shared with the block's own validation. Tests with tampered headers (`HeaderRulesSuite`, `InboundGovernorSuite`: the block fetch server is never asked). Remaining: Byron headers, the first Shelley header after a Byron prefix and the parameter-dependent envelope checks across an epoch boundary are left to the block; no Genesis-style density checks (G3); the throughput effect on Preview is in [performance.md](performance.md).

## N. Ouroboros Leios (v0.3)

Linear Leios (CIP-164) is planned for the Dijkstra hard fork (L1). The design note, sources and
the observed divergences between the CIP text and the prototype are in [leios.md](leios.md).
N2 does not depend on L1 or G4; N3 needs L1 (Dijkstra decoding) and, if the testnet requires it,
G4; N4 needs N3 and a buildable reference Leios node.

- **N1** [#70](https://github.com/KD5UJC-XYZ/scarab/issues/70) (P1, done: design note) Study CIP-164, the Leios formal specification and the reference prototypes; `docs/leios.md` records the revision of every source, the protocol and ledger changes, the mapping onto this codebase, risks, and the plan below. One handshake against the public testnet relay is recorded (versions 14 to 16, magic 164).
- **N2** [#71](https://github.com/KD5UJC-XYZ/scarab/issues/71) (P2) BLS12-381 MinSig keys, proof of possession, votes, aggregation and certificate verification, with the committee order rule, in `core`, pure. Acceptance: strict decoding (subgroup, infinity, canonical); `cardano-base` BLS vectors and the golden `LeiosVote` file with provenance; the 96 versus 48 byte proof-of-possession discrepancy resolved against `dijkstra.cddl`; properties (aggregate of valid votes verifies, any tampering, duplicate or reordered signer, replay on another announcing hash, rogue key all fail); a 900-seat verification benchmark; `blst-java` declared directly and its licence recorded; `check` green; no node change.
- **N3** [#72](https://github.com/KD5UJC-XYZ/scarab/issues/72) (P2, needs L1, maybe G4) Follow the public Leios testnet read-only: LeiosNotify (protocol 18) and LeiosFetch (19) codecs against the Blueprint CDDL with captured fixtures; Dijkstra headers and bodies decoded; certificates verified with N2; EB announcement, body and closure fetched and hash-checked; an evidence row in `compatibility.md` and a precise list of what is skipped. The first mini-protocol exchange with the public relay needs the owner's go-ahead.
- **N4** [#73](https://github.com/KD5UJC-XYZ/scarab/issues/73) (P2, needs N3) A devnet scenario with Haskell Leios nodes and the Scala node: announcements, EBs, votes, certificates and the committee equal to the reference's, ledger state equal at epoch boundaries, the Scala node forging, voting and certifying; throughput and mempool numbers recorded; do-not-fetch and do-not-vote switches. If the reference is not stable enough, stop at a defined scenario plus the divergence list.
- **N5** [#79](https://github.com/KD5UJC-XYZ/scarab/issues/79) (P1) A Leios-grade mempool: 12 MB capacity and per-transaction validation latency against the 1–100 µs target.
